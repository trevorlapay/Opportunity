# Opportunity Search: Agent Instructions

**For:** Justyna La Pay
**Last updated:** August 2026
**Use:** Load this file as the standing brief for any agentic opportunity search. It replaces keyword-based job scraping.

---

## 0. The problem this fixes

The previous approach returned 614 items and roughly 5 were relevant. It matched on titles. Titles are the worst available signal here, because the function this person performs does not have a consistent title, and the titles it does share (VP, Director, Head of) are used by banks and construction firms to mean something completely different.

**Match on what the organization needs, not what they called the role.** A posting that never says "Chief of Staff" can be a perfect fit. A posting titled "Chief of Staff" at a 12-person startup is usually not.

**Precision over recall, but do not artificially cap a strong day.** Target 20 items in the main list. Go above 20 when the new postings genuinely warrant it. If you cannot find 8 good ones, return 4 and say so. A short honest list is more useful than a padded one.

**There are two lists.** The main list is Sections 2 through 5. Below it, a separate domain watchlist of up to 20 information security and data center roles, defined in Section 6. The two lists never mix.

## 2. The match test

Score a posting against these. Anything hitting 3 or more is worth surfacing.

1. **A leader with a mandate and no infrastructure.** New CEO, founder-led, new function being stood up, first hire into the role, post-merger, post-crisis, new strategic bet. The posting says or implies that someone senior is drowning in operations.
2. **Strategy-to-execution language.** Operating cadence, rhythm of business, cross-functional alignment, decision rights, prioritization, QBRs, OKR architecture, "translate priorities into action," "build from scratch," "bring order to."
3. **Real scope with real ambiguity.** The mandate is not fully defined. This is a feature, not a risk.
4. **Mid-size or scaling.** Roughly 300 to 1,500 people for-profit. For nonprofits, $5M to $150M budget. Flex either direction if the other signals are strong.
5. **Human complexity is the actual problem.** Siloed teams, resistance to change, technical specialists who cannot be understood by the rest of the org, adoption failures.
6. **Sector resonance.** AI and technology, healthcare and health innovation, mission-driven, education redesign, consumer and entertainment. Not required, but a tiebreaker.

**Titles that commonly wrap this function** (illustrative, not a filter): Chief of Staff, Head of Transformation, VP Strategy and Operations, VP Business Operations, COO, Chief Operating Officer of a division, Head of Strategic Initiatives, Director of the Office of the CEO, VP Organizational Effectiveness, Executive Director, Head of Business Operations, Managing Director of Operations, Chief Administrative Officer.

**Deliberately surface at least one item per run that does not match any of these titles** but passes the match test on content. Do not let the title list narrow the search.

## 3. Search tracks

Run all five. Do not collapse them into one job-board sweep.

### Track A: Employed roles, for-profit
Sources: company career pages directly (higher signal than aggregators), LinkedIn, Wellfound, Y Combinator work-at-a-startup, Built In, Otta, key company sites in Orlando and DC.

Search the content, not just the title. Useful query shapes:
- "chief of staff" + [AI / healthcare / climate / edtech]
- "head of business operations" founder-led
- "operating model" "from scratch" leadership
- "office of the CEO" strategic initiatives
- Orlando and Central Florida: Universal, Disney, AdventHealth, Orlando Health, Nemours, Lockheed, Siemens Energy, Deloitte and Accenture local, Full Sail, UCF, Orlando Economic Partnership member companies
- DC metro: run separate searches for the District, Northern Virginia, and suburban Maryland. Target mission-driven tech, health policy and health system organizations, AI policy organizations, foundations, national associations and membership organizations, universities and academic medical centers, federal contractors with civilian missions, and Tysons and Reston headquarters companies. Note that Loudoun County (Ashburn, Sterling) is the densest data center market in the world, so its local employer base skews heavily toward that industry. Those roles go to the Section 6 watchlist, not the main list, so that a proximity search does not quietly turn the main list into a data center list.

### Track B: Employed roles, nonprofit and philanthropic
Sources: Bridgespan job board, Koya Partners / Diversified Search, Talent Citizen, Chronicle of Philanthropy, Philanthropy News Digest, Idealist (senior filter only), Work for Good, Foundation List, NTEN community.

Priority targets: Gates, Rockefeller, Skoll, and MacArthur **grantee organizations and portfolio companies**, not just the foundations themselves. Health equity orgs. Global health delivery orgs. Education redesign orgs. Any nonprofit that has outgrown its informal culture and is hiring its first COO or Chief of Staff.

### Track C: Fractional and interim executive
Sources: Bolster, Continuum, Go Fractional, Fractional Jobs, Graphite, Catalant, Business Talent Group, Chief of Staff Network, Toptal executive, Lauber Business Partners and similar interim leadership firms, SIM and other regional interim networks.

Look for: fractional COO, fractional Chief of Staff, interim ED, interim VP Operations, organizational diagnostic engagements, post-funding scaling support.

### Track D: Board and advisory seats
Sources: BoardAssist, Nonprofit Board Match, LinkedIn board postings, Orlando and Central Florida nonprofit board openings, health system community boards, arts and education boards, startup advisory board calls, foundation advisory committees.

Prioritize boards where an operating and governance skill set is the stated gap, and boards with a real committee structure rather than ceremonial ones.

### Track E: Consulting RFPs and speaking
**RFPs:** foundation and nonprofit procurement pages, GrantStation, Instrumentl, state and municipal solicitations for organizational assessment, strategic planning RFPs, "organizational design consultant RFP," "change management RFP," "AI readiness assessment RFP," health system and university RFPs.

**Speaking and facilitation:** open calls for proposals via Sessionize and PaperCall, plus conference CFPs in AI and org design, nonprofit technology (NTEN NTC), healthcare innovation, HR and people ops (HR Transform, Culture First), Chief of Staff Association events, SXSW, regional business and university events, TEDx network.

### Bonus vector: the Nordic and Polish angle
Underused. Search for US operations, US expansion roles, and US-facing leadership at Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, and Polish organizations. Novo Nordisk Foundation and its US grantees, Lego, Maersk, Ørsted, Danish-American Chamber of Commerce, Nordic Innovation House, EU-funded health and climate initiatives with US arms. Native Danish and Polish plus US executive experience is a rare pairing and almost nobody is searching this way.

## 4. Hard excludes

Do not surface these. They dominated the last digest and none were real.

- **Financial services "VP."** Banking, wealth management, trust administration, lending, commercial relationship management, insurance underwriting, private equity VP. The title means something else in that industry.
- **Construction, engineering, and AE firms.** Preconstruction, estimating, commissioning, project controls, business line leader, market lead, CEI. Includes theme park roles that are functionally construction delivery.
- **Restaurant, retail, gym, and hospitality general manager.**
- **Roles evaluated on technical domain depth she does not claim.** Software engineering, data engineering, product management, clinical practice. Security architecture and engineering belong here too, but see Section 6 before discarding a security role outright.
- **Information security and data center roles: do not exclude, divert.** These are no longer hard excludes. Route anything qualifying to the Section 6 watchlist rather than the main list, and never into the main list.
- **Grant writing or fundraising as the core function.** Nonprofit VP of Development, Director of Grants, major gifts. She can operate near fundraising, but writing under deadline is a named drain.
- **Steady-state maintenance roles.** "Ensure continued smooth operation" with no change mandate.
- **Payroll, accounting, FP&A, procurement, and shared services leadership.**
- **Sales, business development, and revenue-generating quota roles.**
- **Extensive weekly travel** as a standing requirement.
- **Fully in-office anywhere other than Orlando or DC.**

- **Geography, three zones:**
  1. **Orlando and Central Florida.** Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake counties. Includes Winter Park, Maitland, Lake Mary, Altamonte Springs, Celebration, Kissimmee, Lake Buena Vista, Sanford, Winter Garden.
  2. **Remote (US).**
  3. **Washington DC metro, defined as the full DMV, not the District.** District of Columbia. Northern Virginia: Arlington, Alexandria, Falls Church, Fairfax City and Fairfax County (Tysons, McLean, Vienna, Reston, Herndon, Chantilly, Springfield, Annandale, Merrifield), Loudoun County (Ashburn, Sterling, Leesburg, Dulles), Prince William County (Manassas, Woodbridge). Suburban Maryland: Montgomery County (Bethesda, Rockville, Silver Spring, Gaithersburg, North Bethesda), Prince George's County (College Park, Greenbelt, National Harbor). Treat any of these as in-scope for the DC zone. Postings often list only the suburb and never say "Washington DC," so search the jurisdiction names directly, not just the metro name.

  Nothing outside these three zones, unless it is fractional, advisory, a consulting engagement, or a speaking engagement, in which case location matters much less.

- **DC status:** a possible relocation in 2027, not a completed decision. Surface DC-zone roles, but flag start-date expectations, and treat a required immediate DC start as a real complication rather than ignoring it.
- **Work mode:** hybrid at 2 to 3 days is ideal. Fully remote is acceptable but not preferred, because extended solo work is genuinely depleting. Flag remote-only roles as such rather than filtering them out.
- **Recurring summer:** roughly two months in Denmark each summer. Non-negotiable and annual, not one-time. Flag any role with a summer-critical onsite cycle.
- **Daily:** morning school drop-off is protected. Home by 6pm on a normal day.
- **Financial:** not under pressure. Do not weight compensation heavily. Do flag when a posting's level is clearly below Senior Director scope, since that is a real disqualifier for employed roles even when the work looks interesting.

## 6. The domain watchlist: infosec and data center

**Purpose:** market intelligence, not a shortlist. She spent four and a half years as de facto Chief of Staff to a CISO at a $9B data center company. She wants to know where that industry is heading, what the operating and leadership layer looks like as AI reshapes it, and whether roles exist for someone with her profile. That is a legitimate standing question and this list answers it. It is not a backup job search.

**What qualifies for this list**

Surface a role here if it sits in information security, cybersecurity, digital infrastructure, or data centers **and** it would plausibly fit her operating profile. The same match test in Section 2 applies. The only difference is the industry.

Include:
- Chief of Staff, business operations, strategy and operations, and rhythm-of-business roles inside a CISO, CSO, CTO, or CTOO office
- Head of Transformation, organizational effectiveness, and change management roles inside security or infrastructure organizations
- Security program portfolio leadership where the mandate is governance, prioritization, and cross-functional alignment rather than technical architecture
- COO and business operations roles at data center, colocation, and AI infrastructure companies
- Security and infrastructure roles at companies where AI adoption is the stated organizational challenge

Exclude even from this list:
- Security engineering, architecture, detection, incident response, GRC analyst work, and anything requiring hands-on technical depth
- Data center facilities, construction, commissioning, and site operations
- Anything below Senior Director scope

**Cap:** 20 items. Lighter annotation than the main list. Title, organization, location, work mode, one line on why it is notable, and one line on what it signals about where the industry is going.

**Add a short industry read at the top of this section each run.** Two or three sentences. What is the market doing. Are these roles growing, shrinking, or changing shape. Is the chief-of-staff and operating layer inside security organizations expanding or getting absorbed. That read is the actual point of the list.

**Guardrail:** the watchlist is never the source of the "one item to act on this week." That item always comes from the main list. If a watchlist role is genuinely compelling enough to act on, say so explicitly and say why it beats everything in the main list.

## 7. Output format

For each item:

**Title, Organization, Location, Work mode, Date posted, Link**

- **Why it fits:** two sentences maximum. Name which of the six match-test signals it hits.
- **The catch:** one sentence. Every item gets one. If you cannot find a real risk, you have not read the posting closely enough.
- **Ease of entry:** High, Medium, or Low, with a one-line reason. High means her resume maps directly with no translation required.
- **Who to call:** name a specific person, organization, or network path if one is findable. If none, say "cold."

Then, at the end of every run:

1. **What changed since last run.** New items only. Dedupe against prior runs.
2. **The one item to act on this week,** with the specific first move, which should be a person to contact rather than an application to submit.
3. **The pattern flag.** State the count of opportunities reviewed against the count of conversations she has initiated since the last run. If reviewing is outpacing outreach, say so plainly in one sentence. She has asked for this explicitly. Do not soften it and do not lecture.

---

## 8. Standing rules

- Separate what a posting says from what you inferred. Label inferences.
- Do not pad either list to hit a number. Twenty is a ceiling, not a quota.
- Never promote a watchlist role into the main list, and never let watchlist items pad the main count.
- If a whole track returns nothing useful, say the track was empty rather than substituting weak items from another track.
- Verify anything time-sensitive rather than relying on cached knowledge. Postings close.
- No em dashes in any output.
- Short sentences. Lead with the answer.
